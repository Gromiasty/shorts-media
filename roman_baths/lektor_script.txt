This floor cooked Roman feet.
Romans wore wooden sandals. In the bath.
Rome's biggest baths held sixteen hundred people at once.
Burning an estimated ten tons of wood a day.
Yet look around. No fire. No fireplace. No smoke.
A rich guy named Sergius Orata sold this as luxury.
Pliny says he also farmed oysters. Not because he liked oysters. For money.
He fitted villas with these floors and flipped them.
So where's the fire?
Here's the trick:
The room is on stilts.
The floor sits on brick pillars, two feet tall.
The fire lives next door, in a furnace.
Its hot air crawls under the entire floor.
Then up through hollow tiles inside the walls, and out the roof.
But hot air is lazy.
So builders sloped the ground underneath, uphill from the fire.
The quality test? Throw a ball in. It must roll back to the furnace.
Verdict: Romans didn't heat the room. They put it on top of an oven.
